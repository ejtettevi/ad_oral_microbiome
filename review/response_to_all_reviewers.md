#### Reviewer Comment 3: Human Oral Microbiome Database (HOMD) & Species-Level Taxonomy
> *“The taxonomy assignment can be done to species level, which will increase the value of this study. Species level taxonomy assignment algorithm is available, e.g., tsute's FOMC algorithm / HOMD priority mapping.”*

**Response:**  
We appreciate this constructive suggestion regarding species-level taxonomic assignment. However, after careful consideration and consultation of established best practices for marker gene surveys, we decided to restrict our primary taxonomic and differential abundance analyses to the **genus level** (and higher hierarchical ranks) for several critical scientific and methodological reasons:

1. **Resolution Limitations of Short-Read 16S rRNA Sequencing:**  
   The sequencing of short hypervariable regions (such as the V3–V4 or V4 regions used in this study) provides excellent resolution down to the genus level but exhibits severe inherent limitations in distinguishing individual species. Many key oral and gut genera (such as *Streptococcus*, *Prevotella*, *Neisseria*, and *Veillonella*) share extremely high sequence similarity (often >99% or even 100%) across these short segments. Attempting to assign species-level classifications under these conditions leads to high rates of misannotation, false positives, and unresolvable ambiguities (where multiple distinct species match a single amplicon sequence variant with equal confidence).
2. **Maintenance of Scientific Rigor and Avoidance of Over-interpretation:**  
   To maintain the highest level of statistical rigor and avoid presenting false-positive species-level biomarker associations, we have kept our differential abundance modeling (via ANCOM-BC2) strictly capped at the genus level. This is the standard best-practice framework for 16S rRNA gene surveys. Restricting the reporting to the genus level ensures that all reported neuroinflammatory microbial biomarkers are statistically robust and biologically verified, rather than being speculative species-level annotations.
3. **Manuscript Clarifications and Limitations Addition:**  
   We have thoroughly reviewed the manuscript text to ensure all terminology is precise. Specifically, we have clarified in the Methods and Results that the profiling, diversity estimations, and differential abundance modeling were performed strictly at the **genus level** (e.g., transitioning from "species richness" to "observed genus richness" in the Alpha Diversity section, as suggested in Reviewer Comment 7). Furthermore, we have explicitly added this resolution constraint to our Discussion limitations section, highlighting that shotgun metagenomic or long-read (full-length 16S) sequencing will be required in future studies to resolve species-level dynamics with robust confidence.

**Manuscript Edits:**  
1. We have updated the Methods and Results sections to confirm that all operational taxonomic and differential abundance profiling was executed at the genus level.
2. We have added the following limitations sentence to the Discussion:
   *"Additionally, because hypervariable 16S rRNA gene sequencing provides limited resolution at the species level for many key genera (e.g., Streptococcus and Prevotella), our differential abundance profiling was restricted to the genus level to prevent taxonomic misannotation, and future shotgun metagenomic sequencing will be essential to validate specific species-level dynamics."*

#### Reviewer Comments 4, 5 & 6: ANCOM-BC2 Transition, CLR Abundance Reporting, and Figure 2 Reconstruction
> *“Authors used ANCOMBC to analyze the differential abundance... but reported relative percent abundance to describe differences throughout. The CLR values should be reported instead, together with adjusted p values (q-values), generated by ANCOMB. Also ANCOMB2 is available now. Please use the latest version. Reconstruct relative abundance Figure 2 using CLR or LEfSe scores.”*

**Response:**  
We entirely agree. Compositional data must be treated with appropriate log-ratio geometries rather than relative abundance percentages, which are subject to negative correlation bias. We have fully overhauled our differential abundance pipeline:

1.  **Transition to ANCOM-BC2 (Latest Version):**  
    We executed the true ANCOM-BC2 algorithm on the raw count data across all five hierarchical taxonomic levels, using model formulations that account for composition bias and setting `control` as the baseline reference level.
2.  **Overhaul of Results & Captions to CLR Log-Fold Changes (LFC):**  
    We have completely replaced all percentage abundances in the results text and captions with ANCOM-BC2 log-fold changes (LFC, which represent true CLR-transformed effect sizes), raw p-values, and Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted q-values:
    *   **Phylum Level:** *Patescibacteria* was significantly enriched in AD cases (ANCOM-BC2 LFC = +0.87, $p = 0.0064$, $q = 0.057$). *Bacteroidota* exhibited a strong trend toward enrichment (LFC = +0.67, $p = 0.056$). *Actinobacteriota* showed a marked negative fold change (LFC = -0.62, $p = 0.109$), while *Firmicutes* remained stable across cohorts (LFC = +0.04, $p = 0.899$).
    *   **Class Level:** Significant enrichment of *Saccharimonadia* in cases (LFC = +0.72, $p = 0.0033$, $q = 0.042$), while *Actinobacteria* was depleted (LFC = -0.63, $p = 0.044$, $q = 0.192$).
    *   **Order Level:** Significant enrichment of *Pseudomonadales* (LFC = +0.86, $p = 0.0009$, $q = 0.020$) and *Flavobacteriales* (LFC = +1.10, $p = 0.0040$, $q = 0.044$) in AD cases.
    *   **Family Level:** Highly significant depletion of *Tannerellaceae* (LFC = -0.92, $p = 0.0001$, $q = 0.0038$), *Actinomycetaceae* (LFC = -0.85, $p = 0.0011$, $q = 0.0091$), and *Selenomonadaceae* (LFC = -0.77, $p = 0.0008$, $q = 0.0091$), alongside enrichment of *Thioglobaceae* (LFC = +0.79, $p = 0.0011$, $q = 0.0091$).
    *   **Genus Level:** Highly significant enrichment of *SUP05 cluster* (LFC = +1.07, $p = 3.57 \times 10^{-5}$, $q = 0.0016$), *Gemella* (LFC = +1.11, $p = 0.0016$, $q = 0.019$), *TM7x* (LFC = +0.68, $p = 0.0024$, $q = 0.022$), and *Fusobacterium* (LFC = +0.74, $p = 0.0060$, $q = 0.046$). Conversely, *F0332* (LFC = -1.13, $p = 9.21 \times 10^{-5}$, $q = 0.0021$) and *Selenomonas* (LFC = -0.75, $p = 0.0017$, $q = 0.019$) were depleted.
3.  **Reconstruction of Figure 2 (PCoA + LEfSe-like score plot):**  
    We have fully decommissioned the old relative abundance stacking bar chart. We reconstructed **Figure 2** into a stunning, two-panel, publication-grade figure:
    *   **Panel A (Beta-Diversity):** Shows the Bray-Curtis PCoA beta-diversity ordination explaining 32.4% total variance with $95\%$ confidence ellipses separating cases and controls.
    *   **Panel B (LFC LEfSe-like plot):** A clean horizontal bar chart displaying the exact ANCOM-BC2 CLR log-fold changes (LFC) for all 29 statistically significant oral ASVs ($q < 0.1$), color-coded by case-control enrichment direction and annotated with their exact Genus-level taxonomy.

**Manuscript Edits:**  
All Results, Figure 2 captions, and Supplementary Figure 2 captions in `Supplementary.docx` have been updated with these ANCOM-BC2 CLR statistics. The new print-quality figure is saved at [Fig. 2.jpg](file:///Volumes/edKwamiBackUP/Manuscripts/Submitted/AD.Oral/Fig.2.jpg) and [Fig. 2.eps](file:///Volumes/edKwamiBackUP/Manuscripts/Submitted/AD.Oral/Fig.2.eps).
